From Natural Language to Exact Cover: A Neuro-Symbolic Approach to Zebra Puzzles
Authors: Paulius Skaisgiris, Thomas Pammer, Veronika Semmelrock, Mykyta Ielanskyi, Maximilian Heisinger, Erich Kobler
First: 2026-11-02T18:58:21+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T18:58:21+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Chain-of-Thought (CoT) generation has substantially improved the performance of Large Language Models (LLMs) on complex reasoning tasks, including code generation, data analysis, and exam-style question answering. Despite these advances, purely neural LLMs continue to struggle with elementary logical reasoning problems and lack the determinism, soundness, and reliability characteristic of symbolic reasoning systems. Conversely, classical symbolic methods such as SAT solving and exact cover guarantee correctness and completeness, but require problems to be expressed in highly specialized formal encodings, limiting their applicability to natural language inputs. In this work, we present a synergistic neuro-symbolic framework that bridges this gap by combining neural semantic parsing with deterministic exact cover reasoning. Given a Zebra-style logic puzzle in natural language, an LLM translates entities and constraints into structured tool calls that construct an exact cover instance. A symbolic solver then computes the solution and provides feedback to iteratively refine invalid, under- or overconstrained encodings, exploiting the uniqueness property of Zebra puzzles. We conduct a comprehensive empirical evaluation across multiple parameter scales, post-training paradigms, and LLM families. The results demonstrate that our hybrid approach consistently outperforms strong plain neural baselines, including CoT prompting, as well as recent neuro-symbolic methods.

Pretraining Data Scale Reverses the Muon-AdamW Fine-Tuning Gap
Authors: Catherine Li, Jingchu Gai, Andrej Risteski, Jacob Mitchell Springer, Aditi Raghunathan
First: 2026-11-02T18:56:43+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T18:56:43+00:00

Abstract
Muon has recently emerged as a promising optimizer for pretraining large language models, offering a speedup over AdamW in minimizing pretraining loss. However, it remains unclear whether Muon's advantage persists after fine-tuning. In this paper, we study the downstream behavior of Muon and AdamW and uncover a surprising trend: beyond a sufficiently large pretraining data budget, AdamW outperforms Muon after fine-tuning. Moreover, as model size increases, AdamW overtakes Muon at progressively smaller pretraining token-parameter ratios. To explain this reversal, we view fine-tuning as a perturbation of the outputs and introduce the margin-to-spread ratio, which measures how far the correct logit lies above the wrong ones relative to how widely the wrong logits are spread. In language models, Muon has the larger margin-to-spread ratio at small data budgets and AdamW at large ones, mirroring the fine-tuning trend. In a linear associative memory, we prove that after one update the margin-to-spread ratio of gradient descent gains on that of Muon as the data budget grows, and simulations show that this trend persists after extended training.

Not All Reasoning Generalizes: A Case Study on Learning Generalizable Reasoning Strategies in RLVR
Authors: Ziqi Pang, Yu-Xiong Wang
First: 2026-11-02T18:53:54+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T18:53:54+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) can enable generalizable improvement for large language model (LLM) reasoning. This paper studies how to incentivize such generalization via reasoning strategies. We study this question with a controllable equation-solving framework: finding multiple inputs x for equations f(x)=y, where f is a non-bijective string function. This setting makes reasoning strategies observable while supporting a clear definition of easy-to-hard out-of-distribution (OOD) shifts, e.g., more solutions, longer y, etc. We uncover a subtle RLVR failure: it teaches the model to solve in-distribution tasks with high accuracy and structured step-by-step derivation, yet under OOD shifts, the same model abandons derivation and collapses to ineffective brute-force enumeration, causing sharp accuracy drops. This observation reveals the deeper challenges of RLVR generalization as it is neither incapable base model nor reward hacking, since the model succeeds in-distribution with the correct reasoning strategy. The key to mitigating this failure is an indirect intervention that surprisingly helps: co-training with bijective inversion tasks. Although these bijective tasks are actually not uniformly harder and even further from the non-bijective OOD evaluation, they make derivation more certain and substantially restore the correct strategy and accuracy under OOD shifts. This effect further motivates our theoretical analysis: outcome-only RLVR can shape reasoning processes through implicitly crediting the effective reasoning strategies more, even without process supervision. To conclude, our analysis framework and findings systematically reveal the crucial role and incentivization of reasoning strategies in RLVR generalization under OOD shifts. Code is available at https://github.com/ziqipang/rl_meta_thinking.

Isekai3D SDG: A Platform for Diverse Synthetic Gameplay Visual Data
Authors: Rafal Karp, Gene Liu, Shih-Hsin Li, Eric Courtemanche, Shengze Wang, Josef Spjut, David Pankratz, Mischa Kolbe, Michael Stengel, Sven Elflein, Adam Grygielski, Liwen Wu, Anjul Patney

Abstract
We present Isekai3D Synthetic Data Generation (SDG), a platform for simulating and capturing synthetic 3D gameplay for research in vision and world models, neural graphics, gameplay agents, and other applications. The platform combines reusable environment and character assets with Unreal Engine plugins in a two-stage workflow. First, world simulation generates gameplay while recording the world state, character movements, actions, and camera tracks needed for replay. Second, the recorded simulation is replayed to capture selected outputs, such as rendered video, images, and depth maps, at a specified resolution and frame rate under selected rendering and camera configurations. Separating simulation from capture allows the same recorded gameplay to produce multiple output variants without repeating the original simulation. Visual outputs are accompanied by frame-aligned camera, character, and action metadata. The platform uploads generated data directly in a training-friendly format, reducing intermediate storage, preparation time, and manual processing. This report describes the design of our overall system, including the platform's assets, plugins, and their functionality, explaining how they work together to generate diverse synthetic gameplay data.

D&A: Decoupling Description and Assessment Enables VLMs as Zero-Shot Robotic Reward Models
Authors: Marius Memmel, Shawn Yin, Urvi Rutia, Angad Wadhwa, Eldon Li, Gavin Angeloff, Ankit Goyal, Jesse Zhang, Dieter Fox, Fabio Ramos, Anqi Li, Abhishek Gupta
First: 2026-11-02T18:52:11+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T18:52:11+00:00
Comments: Website at https://weirdlabuw.github.io/dna
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) hold promise for zero-shot video task-progress estimation in robot learning, yet existing frameworks frequently suffer from confirmation bias, hallucinating progress in failed executions due to implicit success assumptions or relative temporal ranking designs. Naive prompting of frontier models collapses into a binary success detector that fails to capture nuanced, non-linear progress and failures. While post-training approaches can mitigate this through explicit training, they demand expensive real-world robotics data and require computationally intensive post-training for every individual base model. To resolve these limitations, we introduce D&A (Describe & Assess): our approach decouples progress estimation into an objective, task-grounded textual description of scene dynamics followed by an assessment phase using explicit rubrics. By compelling the VLM to synthesize an explicit, text-based definition of absolute success before scoring, the framework anchors evaluations to an external, global criterion rather than the video's internal timeline. Empirical results demonstrate that our method calibrates zero-shot estimates and mitigates confirmation bias, providing fine-grained, partial progress metrics that accurately penalize failed executions while correctly interpolating successful runs. Being purely prompt-based, D&A generalizes across tasks, embodiments, and benchmarks---matching or surpassing post-trained reward models---and its reward estimates enable efficient downstream reinforcement learning.

Abstract

Abstract
Most parameters of a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model belong to its experts, and standard training keeps every expert gradient in GPU memory until the optimizer step. Abstract
The quadratic scaling of attention is a fundamental bottleneck limiting the long-context capabilities of large language models (LLMs). A variety of approaches have attempted to circumvent this, but they either fail to match attention's expressiveness (e.g., sliding-window or linear attention variants with fixed-size state) or only achieve constant-factor speedups (e.g., sparse attention methods whose indexers themselves scale quadratically). In this paper, we derive an asymptotically subquadratic sparse attention method based on a hierarchical, tree-structured indexer, which we call Zyphra Hierarchical Attention (ZHA). ZHA constructs a tree of recursively compressed key summaries over the KV cache, which future queries traverse from the top down to retrieve a sparse subset of keys. Crucially, by iteratively growing the tree's depth, the total cost including routing and final attention grows sublinearly in sequence length, resulting in overall subquadratic (but superlinear) scaling for attention. We demonstrate that ZHA can be cheaply applied to existing pretrained models with only a short KL distillation training stage of a billion tokens, and maintains performance on long-context retrieval and challenging reasoning, mathematical, and agentic coding evaluation tasks. Moreover, we show that our method is highly robust to tree geometry including very deep trees of up to 6 levels. Finally, we develop a detailed performance model and show that our approach scales subquadratically, dramatically outperforms competing methods beyond 1 million tokens of context, and ultimately has the potential to extend effective context windows to the hundreds of millions or billions of tokens.

Abstract
Exemplar-free continual learning on pre-trained Vision Transformers increasingly relies on Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), yet prevailing methods govern the low-rank update space through strict subspace isolation. By projecting each incoming task into the approximate null space of historical token activations, isolation-based learners equate any cross-task feature overlap with catastrophic interference. We show that this orthogonality premise induces a structural bottleneck we term \emph{spectral starvation}: when down-projection bases are locked before task optimization, the gradient norm reaching the trainable up-projection is upper-bounded by the energy of current-task features captured by the chosen basis. Because natural visual tasks share low- and mid-level covariance structure, historical null tails contain barely $5.5\%$ of incoming task variance, starving new-class plasticity while blocking positive consolidation along shared directions. We propose BISECT (Bilevel Spectral Decomposition), a continual LoRA framework that partitions a fixed rank-$r$ budget into two orthogonal functional blocks: an invariant carrier spanned by the dominant modes of the joint historical-and-current Gram matrix, and a novelty complement extracted via generalized Rayleigh whitening followed by orthogonal complement projection. After single-task training, BISECT folds both blocks back into the frozen backbone using a closed-form precision gate that scales each rank-1 invariant atom by its current-to-joint Rayleigh ratio while preserving novelty atoms at unit gain. Across eight incremental settings on ImageNet-R, ImageNet-A, CIFAR-100, CUB-200, and DomainNet, BISECT outperforms prompt, adapter, and LoRA baselines with zero extra inference parameters or FLOPs, and remains complementary to sparse analytic expansion.

Abstract

Abstract
Skin cancer diagnosis, particularly differentiating melanoma from benign nevi, is challenging due to the visual similarity between lesions. Although convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and vision transformers (ViTs) have shown promising performance, their effectiveness often deteriorates on data from heterogeneous clinical sources. Conventional domain adaptation methods address domain shift but require access to source data during adaptation, which is often infeasible due to privacy regulations. Multi-source-free unsupervised domain adaptation (MSFDA) addresses this limitation by leveraging multiple labeled source domains to generalize to an unlabeled target domain without requiring access to source data. However, existing MSFDA methods rely on full backbone fine-tuning, leading to catastrophic forgetting and overfitting on small clinical datasets, and address domain shift at the aggregation stage without establishing a shared domain-invariant feature space. Furthermore, their reliance on hard pseudo-labels or confidence-weighted aggregation introduces noisy supervision signals under domain shift. To address these limitations, we propose CAT-CKD, consisting of two components: (1) contrastive adapter training (CAT), which trains lightweight ConvPass adapters within a frozen ViT backbone using supervised contrastive learning (SCL) to establish a shared domain-invariant feature space before source-specific model training, and (2) consensus knowledge distillation (CKD), which aggregates logits from multiple source models into a consensus supervisory signal and adapts a student model on unlabeled target data using KL divergence. Experiments on five publicly available skin lesion datasets show that CAT-CKD achieves an average AUROC of 86.1%, outperforming existing MSFDA methods while requiring only 4.3M trainable parameters. Abstract
Organisations increasingly deploy large language model agents that must follow organisational values. We check whether activation steering, which adds vectors to a model's internal activations during generation without changing its weights, can improve compliance beyond prompting alone. Since most scenarios involve several values at once, we focus on multi-value steering. A simple way to build these vectors is to contrast responses that follow all values relevant to a scenario with responses that violate them. However, this gives nearly identical vectors for different values: they capture a general compliance signal rather than individual values, which limits their use when combining several values. We propose Value-Isolating Activation Steering (VIAS), which contrasts responses to the same scenario under two prompts, one listing all relevant values and one omitting a single target value. We keep a pair only if the target value changes from compliant to non-compliant and both responses still meet a minimum compliance level on the other values. We evaluate VIAS on PROTECT, a workplace compliance dataset where each scenario is tagged with relevant values from a 21-value taxonomy. VIAS reduces overlap between value vectors, making them better suited for multi-value steering, although this alone does not lead to independent control over each value. Combined with prompting, VIAS complies with every tagged value on 69.6% of scenarios for Qwen2.5-7B, 75.2% for Mistral-7B, and 72.6% for Phi-3.5-mini, outperforming prompting alone for all three models.

Abstract
Parameter-efficient continual learning on pre-trained Vision Transformers largely relies on a costly premise: preventing catastrophic forgetting requires either spawning a dedicated LoRA branch per session---causing adapter parameters to scale linearly with task horizon---or applying sparse Mixture-of-Experts gating symmetrically to both forward and backward passes, which sacrifices representational rank at test time. We argue that reading consolidated knowledge and writing new task credit are fundamentally asymmetric operations. Because visual features in a shared low-rank space distribute across rank-1 atoms rather than localizing in disjoint task silos, forward inference should read all atoms at full rank capacity, whereas backward optimization should restrict plasticity to a sparse subset of input-aligned channels. We introduce RASP (Read-All, Sparse-Plasticity LoRA), which adapts a single stationary-size adapter across sequential tasks. At each step, a scene-modulated cosine router selects the top-$q$ unlocked atoms to receive gradients, blocks updates on the remaining $r-q$ channels, and penalizes Frobenius Gram coherence between active and non-updated output directions. Across sessions, the router's empirical activation trace and each atom's exact spectral norm form a zero-overhead geometric consolidation index $ω_j = \sqrt{\tilde{c}_j \tildeσ_j}$ that drives Cauchy step attenuation and boundary anchor locking. Across CIFAR-100, CUB-200, ImageNet-R, and ImageNet-A, RASP matches or surpasses branch-expanding LoRA learners under a strictly constant parameter footprint.

Abstract
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems rely critically on the retriever module to surface relevant context for large language models. Although numerous retrievers have recently been proposed, each built on different ranking principles such as lexical matching, dense embeddings, or graph citations, there remains a lack of systematic understanding of how these mechanisms differ and overlap. We introduce principled metrics grounded in information theory to quantify retrieval quality, redundancy/synergy, and marginal contribution. Although the framework is designed to be applicable to any setting where a ground-truth answer can construct a pseudo chunk distribution, the present empirical study focuses on multi-hop question answering - a demanding setting where retriever complementarity is especially pronounced. Abstract
Speculative decoding speeds up large language model (LLM) serving only while the GPU has spare compute, so the best draft length, the number of tokens a small drafter guesses per step, shrinks as the batch grows. vLLM lets operators set the draft length for each batch size with a hand-written table, but nothing measures or corrects that table. We present TableTune. Its tuner times the decoding step for every batch size and draft length on vLLM's own serving path and computes, in minutes, the table that maximizes throughput. Its controller, a vLLM plugin, tracks how many guesses are accepted in live traffic and rebuilds the table while serving. We measure TableTune only against other ways to speculate. On an H100, the tuned table delivers at least 94% of the throughput of the best fixed draft length at every load in four of five drafter settings (88% in the fifth) without the operator having to know which length that is. Against reimplementations of TurboSpec- and Nightjar-style control on the same engine, its worst-case loss is never higher than theirs, and with the EAGLE-3 drafter it is 3% against 9% and 12%. On a mixture-of-experts model, vLLM's documented table loses up to 18% and the tuned table at most 3%. Starting from mistuned tables, the controller raises throughput by up to 58% (up to 45% on vLLM's default runner), and after a workload switch with the DFlash drafter it recovers 96% of a stale table's loss. In llm-d's optimized-baseline deployment as shipped, two Qwen3-32B replicas on four GPUs, the tuned table is up to 32% faster than a fixed draft length of 3 and up to 25% faster than vLLM's documented table at high load, and the controller raises the documented table's throughput by up to 29%.

Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) achieve strong performance in long-horizon agentic tasks through multi-step interaction and reasoning at test time. However, repeatedly invoking these models incurs substantial inference cost over an episode. Post-training quantization (PTQ) reduces this cost, but using a fixed low precision throughout an episode can sharply lower task success. We observe that interaction steps differ sharply in their sensitivity to quantization: given the same context, decoding a step at low precision turns a success into a failure at only about 1% of the steps, which we call critical steps. Based on this observation, we propose Dynamic Mixed-Precision Routing (DMR), a framework that adaptively selects between high-precision and low-precision versions of the same LLM at each decision step. Leveraging an entire-space factorization to jointly predict a step's divergence and its effect on the outcome, DMR learns to recognize these critical steps by training only a lightweight router on the model's hidden state. By reusing a shared high-precision KV cache and the hidden states, DMR avoids redundant context prefill and a separate text encoder, enabling precision switching with less than 1 ms of overhead per step and adding less than 0.11% to step latency for routing. This mixed-precision design also enables nested quantization, reducing the memory footprint by 39% on average compared with FP16. Across three long-horizon agentic benchmarks with nested quantization, DMR achieves an average 2.44x decode speedup over FP16 at comparable task success rates, without fine-tuning the underlying model. On ALFWorld with GPTQ, DMR achieves a 1.71x decode speedup with 86.9% success, compared with 87.2% for FP16.

Abstract
Qualitative insights from user experiences are critical for informing product and policy decisions, but scaling qualitative data collection is constrained by the availability of trained interviewers. Recent work has explored using large language models (LLMs) to automate interviewing, yet existing systems lack a principled mechanism for balancing systematic coverage of predefined topics with adaptive exploration, or the ability to pursue follow-ups, deep dives, and emergent themes that arise organically during conversation. In this work, we formulate adaptive semi-structured interviewing as an optimization problem over the interviewer's behavior. We define interview utility as a trade-off between coverage of a predefined interview topic guide, discovery of relevant emergent themes, and interview cost measured by length. Based on this formulation, we introduce SparkMe, a multi-agent LLM interviewer that performs deliberative planning via simulated conversation rollouts to select questions with high expected utility. We evaluate SparkMe through controlled experiments with LLM-based interviewees, showing that it achieves higher interview utility, improving topic guide coverage ($+4.7\%$ over the best baseline) and eliciting richer emergent insights while using fewer conversational turns than prior LLM interviewing approaches. We further validate SparkMe in a user study with $70$ participants across $7$ professions, where occupational domain experts rate it as producing higher-quality, more adaptive interviews and validate profession-specific insights not captured by the baseline. In addition, separately recruited experts in qualitative or human-centered research also find stronger responsiveness and probing in SparkMe. The code and evaluation resources for SparkMe are available as open source at https://github.com/SALT-NLP/SparkMe.

Abstract
Foundational vision models have become the de facto standard for many vision tasks due to their strong performance. However, they are notoriously opaque and remain hard to interpret. We present ALOE (ALign Once to Explain), a one-time, label-free feature alignment approach that efficiently converts foundational vision models into inherently interpretable B-cos variants. Once aligned, the B-cos backbone is used as a drop-in replacement across several downstream tasks-amortizing the cost of interpretability. ALOE is robust across pre-training paradigms (supervised, self-supervised, vision-language) and can preserve teacher-level generalization with orders of magnitude less data than training from scratch. On classification, it strongly outperforms vanilla B-cosification (e.g., +10.5 p.p. top-1 on ImageNet for DINOv3 ViT-B/16), retains strong linear probing, k-NN, and zero-shot transfer competitive with foundational backbones (DINOv3, SigLIP2) across diverse downstream datasets. It also preserves spatially structured features useful for dense prediction, while yielding well-localized and highly human-interpretable explanations by design.

Abstract
A strand of mechanistic approaches to deception in large language models (LLMs) relies on "lie detectors" - truth probes aimed to identify model outputs that are internally represented as false. This lie detector approach to LLM deception implicitly assumes that deception is coextensive with lying. Abstract

Abstract

Abstract

Abstract
Geospatial text spotting involves locating and transcribing geographic labels, such as road names, district names, and landmarks, in raster maps. These labels are often small, densely arranged, and arbitrarily oriented. Although Arabic text recognition has been studied extensively in documents and natural scenes, Arabic geospatial text spotting remains underexplored. We introduce ARABGEOTEXT, an Arabic-centric benchmark covering 970 geographic locations in Riyadh and Jeddah, with 17,875 manually annotated text instances per view type. For each location, we pair an OpenStreetMap (OSM) rendering with a composite created by overlaying that rendering on the corresponding remote sensing (RS) imagery. The paired views preserve label content and pixel geometry, allowing evaluation across different backgrounds. Geographic partitions support in-city and city-disjoint evaluation of detection, recognition, and end-to-end spotting. Experiments with direct Qwen3.5-4B spotting show difficulties in recovering all labels within a tile. We therefore propose a tool-augmented vision-language framework that combines YOLO26 localization and crop rectification with Qwen3.5-4B recognition adapted through low-rank adaptation (LoRA). The framework outperforms direct LoRA-adapted Qwen3.5-4B spotting across both image views and test cities. With localization held fixed, reader adaptation further improves recognition over the pretrained zero-shot reader. The dataset and code will be made publicly available at https://github.com/yakoubbazi/ArabGeoText.

Abstract
World action models have demonstrated promising performance in robot policy learning, yet their high inference latency remains a critical bottleneck for real-world deployment. Recent approaches have sought to improve inference efficiency by eliminating test-time future-frame imagination or accelerating action prediction. However, aggregating representations from all video world model layers can introduce redundant computation, while standard backpropagation may distribute action-supervision gradients unevenly across layers, limiting the effectiveness of world representation learning. In this work, we propose TurboWAM, a fast world action model that learns to efficiently aggregate and optimize world representations for action prediction. Specifically, TurboWAM introduces an efficient world representation aggregation mechanism that restricts aggregation to a bounded range of relevant world model layers identified through layer relevance analysis. Within this bounded range, TurboWAM estimates aggregation weights for different world model layers based on the current action denoising state and combines them with learnable layer-wise weights to fuse world representations. Moreover, TurboWAM employs a world model gradient modulation mechanism that decomposes action-supervision gradients into a layer-shared component and layer-specific residual components, enabling more balanced gradient propagation across world model layers. This mechanism operates exclusively during training and introduces no additional inference computation. Together, these designs enable more efficient world representation learning and low-latency action prediction. Extensive experiments on multiple robot manipulation benchmarks and real-world manipulation tasks show that TurboWAM achieves low inference latency while maintaining competitive task success rates compared with state-of-the-art methods.

Authors: Bat-Sheva Einbinder, Hen Davidov, Yee Whye Teh, Yarin Gal, Yaniv Romano

Abstract
While large language models (LLMs) are trained to align with human values, their generations may still violate safety constraints. A growing line of work addresses this problem by modifying the model's sampling policy at decoding time using a safety score. However, existing decoding-time steering methods often intervene unnecessarily, modifying generations that would have been safe under the base model. Such unnecessary interventions are undesirable, as they can distort key properties of the base model such as helpfulness, fluency, style, and coherence. We propose a new test-time steering method designed to reduce such unnecessary interventions while improving the safety of responses. Our approach filters tokens using a value-based safety criterion and provides an explicit bound on the probability of false interventions. A single threshold hyperparameter controls this bound, allowing practitioners to trade off higher rates of unnecessary intervention for better output safety. Across multiple datasets and experiments, we show that our value-filtered decoding method outperforms existing baselines, achieving better trade-offs between safety, helpfulness, and similarity to the base model.

Abstract
Generative recommendation formulates next-item prediction as autoregressive generation over semantic ID (SID) sequences derived from users' historical interactions, making modern recommender systems structurally similar to large language models (LLMs). As privacy and safety concerns grow, these systems increasingly require concept unlearning to remove sensitive or harmful concepts associated with items. However, existing LLM unlearning methods cannot be directly applied to generative recommendation. Unlike word tokens with explicit semantics, SIDs are abstract identifiers that are often shared by both forget and retain items, leading to severe conflicts between concept removal and recommendation utility preservation. Rather than directly suppressing shared SIDs, TRACER reassigns concept-related items to alternative tokens that better facilitate forgetting while minimizing side effects on retained items. Experiments on real-world recommendation datasets demonstrate that TRACER effectively removes target concepts while substantially better preserving recommendation utility than existing unlearning baselines.

Abstract
Robotic manipulation is inherently geometric, but what shape does the world take inside the policy that performs it? Prior work on vision-language-action (VLA) policies establishes that task-relevant variables are linearly readable from activations, yet readability certifies only that a variable is present and says nothing about the shape its values trace, which may be curved and multi-dimensional. Using controlled sweeps, natural rollouts, linear probes, and activation interventions, we build a taxonomy of seven task-relevant concepts in VLA models. Overall, this work lays out an initial phenomenology of realized geometry in VLA policies and demonstrates that a concept's shape carries as much importance as its readability.

Abstract
Text-to-SQL translates natural language questions into SQL queries, enabling database access without writing SQL. Fine-tuning open-weight large language models (LLMs) is cost-effective, but existing methods treat SQL as ordinary text and optimize token-level likelihood without explicitly modeling its syntactic, semantic, and logical requirements. Thus, a single incorrect alias or comparison operator can invalidate a query while contributing little to training loss. We propose SQLoom, a constraint-aware fine-tuning framework with supervision at three levels: syntactic validity under SQL grammar and database schema, semantic alignment between query decisions and the question, and logical consistency across dependent clause decisions. SQLoom extends supervised fine-tuning with a unified set-contrastive objective favoring sets of correct outputs over minimally different incorrect ones at each level. LLM agents propose this supervision, which a SQL parser and execution on counterexample databases verify. We also release Spider-Refined, a training dataset correcting 378 flawed Spider annotations and enriching database schemas. Experiments on Spider, BIRD, and three perturbed Spider variants show that SQLoom improves execution accuracy for Qwen3-1.7B from 62.9% zero-shot to 88.7% on Spider development, reaching 89.2% on Spider test and 73.4% on BIRD development. It outperforms fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, and agent-based baselines with up to 70 billion parameters. On question perturbations, SQLoom loses at most 0.6 accuracy points versus up to 12.1 for agent-based methods. It generates SQL in a single pass at 0.42s per query, 23$\times$ faster than a GPT-4-based agent method. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of set-contrastive supervision over token-level training.

Abstract
Zeroth-order (ZO) optimization enables memory-efficient large language model (LLM) fine-tuning, and recent methods incorporate curvature-aware strategies. However, in the high-dimensional parameter space of LLMs, these methods inherently suffer from exploding gradient variance and prohibitive costs. Conversely, confining updates to a low-dimensional subspace reduces variance but discards critical high-dimensional information, degrading performance. This creates a fundamental dilemma: full-space optimization is informative but has high variance, while subspace optimization has low variance but loses information. To break this trade-off, we propose ENGINE (Endogenous Variational MultiScale). Inspired by computational mechanics, we adapt static Variational MultiScale (VMS) theory to dynamic optimization, treating low- and high-dimensional spaces as resolved coarse and unresolved fine scales. Through a multigrid-style V-cycle, ENGINE alternates between spaces, dynamically probing fine-grained information in the high-dimensional space and injecting it to correct the efficient low-dimensional sampling process. Under appropriate assumptions, statistical analysis characterizes variance reduction and correction of curvature-induced bias, and convergence analysis establishes a stationarity bound for the corrected coarse updates. Evaluations on standard LLM benchmarks show that ENGINE achieves higher accuracy and faster convergence than existing ZO methods, achieving up to a $2.61\times$ speed-up in forward passes with zero additional peak memory overhead. Code is available at https://github.com/AI4Science-WestlakeU/ENGINE.
